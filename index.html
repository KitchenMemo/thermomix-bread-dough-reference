Quick reference

  • Baseline ratio: 500 g bread flour : 300–325 g water : 7 g instant yeast : 8–10 g salt : 10–15 g oil (optional). In baker’s percentages that is 60–65% water, 1.4% yeast, 1.6–2% salt, 2–3% oil.
  • Hydration feel: 55–58% firm • 60–65% soft and slightly tacky • 70%+ wet and sticky.
  • Warm liquid: about 37°C (99°F) is a common starting point, not a requirement. Avoid hot liquid, which can damage yeast.
  • Kneading: follow your model and recipe. Cookidoo equates 2 minutes of TM6 Dough mode to roughly 10 minutes of hand kneading.
  • What Dough mode does not do: it does not prove or bake. TM6 and TM7 offer Fermentation mode as an optional proving route.
  • Limits: TM6 dough-mode quantity is 1 kg. TM7 Dough mode is unavailable at a bowl temperature of 60°C or higher. Check your own manual.
  • Tests: windowpane test = gluten development. Poke test = final proof.
  • Sticky dough: do not add flour straight away. Rest it, then reassess.
  • First proof: commonly 1–2 hours, depending on temperature, hydration, yeast and flour.

Troubleshooting matrix

Find a symptom, its likely causes, the fix and how to prevent it.

SymptomStagePossible causePractical fixPrevention
Sticky doughMixing High hydration, flour that absorbs differently, or not enough rest Give the flour time to hydrate and reassess before adding any flour. If still very loose, check the weights against the recipe. Weigh ingredients; use the same flour each time.
Dense loafProving Weak gluten development, under-proving, too much flour, or low yeast activity Check dough structure and allow enough proving time. Avoid adding unnecessary flour. Do not blame kneading alone; hydration and proving matter equally.
Dough not risingProving Inactive yeast, unsuitable liquid temperature, or a cold environment Check the yeast, then move the dough somewhere warmer. Do not immediately add more yeast. Store yeast correctly and check its expiry date.
Dry or tight doughMixing Too little water or too much flour Reassess hydration and correct gradually if the formula allows. Weigh flour instead of using cups.
Flat loaf after bakingBaking Over-proving, weak dough structure, or unsuitable hydration Review the final proof and shaping method. Check the dough before baking instead of relying on a timer. Use the poke test and keep the process consistent.
Dough climbing the bowl or feeling unusually warmMachine Batch too large, unusually warm dough, or unsuitable kneading duration Stop the cycle and check the batch against your model’s instructions. Reduce the batch if overloaded. Use appropriate quantities; do not experiment beyond model limits or leave experimental batches unattended.

Storage and make-ahead

MethodWhat to know
Refrigerate unbaked doughCovered dough can be chilled for slower fermentation if the formula supports cold proving. Cold dough may need extra time to finish expanding, so check it before baking.
Freeze shaped doughPossible in portions, but yeast activity and rise can be reduced. Wrap securely, label, thaw in the fridge, then complete proving. Less reliable than baking fresh.
Store baked breadCool completely, then keep in a bread bag or container at room temperature. Freeze sliced portions for longer storage.
Scaling upDo not simply double flour and water. Check the model’s dough limit and make separate batches when needed.
Scaling downKeep the formula’s proportions and weigh accurately.
Food safetyDo not taste raw dough: raw flour can carry harmful bacteria. Cook dough thoroughly and wash hands and surfaces after handling raw flour. Discard dough with visible mould, odd discolouration or other signs of contamination.
